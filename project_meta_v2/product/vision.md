# Revoke 2.0 Vision

## Product thesis

Revoke exists to help people change compulsive app behavior through explicit behavioral Commitments and the systems required to keep them when motivation changes.

Revoke is a behavioral commitment and rehabilitation system. The Android blocker is the enforcement engine, not the product itself.

## Core loop

Observe -> Commit -> Plan -> Enforce -> Override -> Learn -> Adapt

Observe establishes behavioral reality. Commit defines the contract. Plan creates Reduce stages or Protect boundaries. Enforce makes the contract harder to break. Override is deliberate and bounded. Learn exposes adherence, overrides, slips, and recovery. Adapt changes only future plans or an explicitly replaced contract.

## Primary product object

The primary object is a Commitment. Schedules, time blocks, usage limits, taper stages, reminders, and native restrictions are implementation mechanisms generated from or controlled by a Commitment.

A Commitment answers what behavior is changing, which apps are involved, baseline and target, dates, enforcement, override authority, optional Circle participation, optional Credit backing, and what counts as verified success, verified failure, unverifiable evidence, grace, and settlement.

## Commitment forms

### Reduce

Progressively lower usage from a measured baseline to a target through explicit stages/checkpoints.

### Protect

Establish a hard boundary such as staying off an app during a period or maintaining a daily maximum. Initial native mechanisms are Time Block and Usage Limit. Launch Count is outside Revoke 2.0.

## Principles

1. Commitments are explicit contracts; Revoke never silently changes targets, criteria, Credits, or authority.
2. Enforcement is local-first; activated commitments continue where Android permits even if Firebase or Flutter is temporarily unavailable.
3. Financial consequences require stronger evidence than ordinary behavioral friction.
4. Uncertainty favors the user: UNVERIFIABLE returns Credits and never consumes grace.
5. Accountability Circles are optional and least-privilege.
6. Circle membership never grants full profile or unrelated usage access.
7. Slips are not abandonment; recovery is a first-class behavior.
8. AI executes approved policy and cannot change an active contract.
9. Premium and Commitment Credits are distinct. Credits are purchased through Google Play, remain inside Revoke, and are not cash or prizes.
10. Google Play policy compatibility and legal assumptions require final external validation before release.

## What Revoke 2.0 is not

- a generic productivity timer;
- a public social network or leaderboard loop;
- a punishment-first product;
- an investment, prize, or money-making product;
- a payment product disguised as digital wellness;
- an autonomous AI coach that changes commitments;
- a full rewrite of working native enforcement for stylistic reasons.
